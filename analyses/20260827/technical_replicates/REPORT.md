# Technical-replicate reproducibility — 20260630 vs 20260721

Baseline for reading every cross-experiment number. Generated by `replicate_report.py`;
tables in `outputs/`, figure in `outputs/figures/t01_replicate_reproducibility.svg`.

**On the two columns.** ρ is rank agreement; R² is one replicate taken as a literal prediction
of the other, scored against the mean (`1 − Σ(y−x)²/Σ(y−ȳ)²`), so it also charges for offset and
slope. ρ is the column to compare *across modalities* — OD is plate-z-scored and relative
abundance lives in [0,1], so their R² denominators are not the same thing. R² is here because it
is the scale on which the genomic-ML results are reported; **but neither column is on the ML
target.** For a ceiling that can be set directly against a cv_strain R², see §6.

**These are cross-plate replicates.** 96% of pairs with ≥2 wells have them on *different*
destination plates, so this measures the whole pipeline — pipetting, plate, position, reader
session — not well-to-well noise on one plate. Replicate counts: 20260630 has 2985 pairs at
2× and 1020 at 3×; 20260721 has 2190 at 2× and 1551 at 3×.

20260721's sequencing uses the post-`MIN_RESOLVABLE_BP` re-run (`relative_abundance_refix`).

## 1. Both assays are reproducible — and sequencing beats the plate reader

Split-half agreement (first replicate vs mean of the rest), on the **same set of pairs** for
both modalities:

| | plate reader OD600 | sequencing (rel. abundance) |
|---|---|---|
| **20260630** | ρ = 0.869, R² = 0.759 | **ρ = 0.928, R² = 0.875** (median \|Δ\| = 0.040) |
| **20260721** | ρ = 0.686, R² = 0.302 | **ρ = 0.825, R² = 0.675** (median \|Δ\| = 0.087) |

R² separates the two experiments far more harshly than ρ does: 20260721's OD keeps a respectable
rank agreement (0.686) while explaining only 30% of the variance. Rank order survives noise that
destroys magnitude.

Sequencing wins in both experiments despite only ~40 reads per well. A replicate pair of wells
agrees on relative abundance to within 0.04 (20260630) or 0.09 (20260721).

## 2. 20260721 is the noisier experiment, in both modalities independently

OD 0.686 vs 0.869; sequencing 0.825 vs 0.928. Two unrelated measurements agree that 20260721
is the lower-quality run. Any comparison involving it inherits that.

## 3. Wavelength barely matters — OD600 is fine

Across all 61 wavelengths (350–950 nm, 10 nm steps):

| wavelength | 20260630 ρ | 20260721 ρ | 20260630 R² | 20260721 R² | median raw OD (20260630) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 350 nm | 0.873 | 0.683 | **0.786** | 0.428 | 1.124 |
| **410 nm (best ρ)** | **0.878** | **0.692** | 0.775 | **0.430** | 0.753 |
| 500 nm | 0.873 | 0.688 | 0.739 | 0.416 | 0.490 |
| **600 nm (convention)** | 0.869 | 0.687 | 0.708 | 0.402 | 0.345 |
| 700 nm | 0.863 | 0.685 | 0.679 | 0.386 | 0.257 |
| 950 nm (worst) | 0.835 | 0.668 | 0.588 | 0.322 | 0.189 |

**The "flat curve" conclusion is a ρ result and does not fully survive in R².** On ρ, 410 nm
beats 600 nm by only +0.009 / +0.005 — negligible. On R² the same sweep drops 0.786 → 0.588
across the range, and the blue end beats 600 nm by **+0.078** (20260630, peak at 350 nm) and
+0.029 (20260721, peak at 360 nm). Rank agreement is nearly wavelength-independent; agreement in
*magnitude* is not, and tracks raw signal level, which falls ~6× from 350 to 900 nm.

So the recommendation splits by use. **If OD is a covariate or a ranking, stay on OD600** — the
conclusion is unchanged. If an OD value is used quantitatively (as it is in `genomic_ml_yield`,
which regresses on plate-z-scored OD600), the blue end is worth ~0.08 R² for free, with the
caveat that blue picks up media absorbance as well as scattering, so a media-only blank matters
more there.

## 4. Sequencing depth is the lever that does matter

Stratified by each pair's *minimum* well depth (a replicate comparison is only as good as its
shallower member):

| pair min depth | 20260630 ρ (R²) | 20260721 ρ (R²) | 20260721 median \|Δ\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| <25 reads | 0.920 / 0.846 (n=68) | 0.723 / 0.478 (n=192) | 0.124 |
| 25–45 | 0.916 / 0.859 (n=165) | 0.885 / 0.827 (n=169) | 0.067 |
| ≥45 | 0.969 / 0.947 (n=57) | 0.905 / 0.830 (n=82) | 0.057 |

20260630 is already near ceiling at any depth. **20260721 gains a lot from depth** — ρ 0.72 → 0.91,
and error halves — because 43% of its replicate pairs sit below 25 reads. More reads per well is
the highest-value change for a repeat experiment.

## 5. What this does to the cross-experiment result

The shared-pair cross-experiment correlation was **ρ = 0.084**. Attenuation from measurement
noise alone would cap a perfect match at roughly √(0.869 × 0.686) ≈ **0.77**.

So the cross-experiment comparison is *not* noise-limited. If the two experiments' wells held
the same pairs, the expected correlation is ~0.77 and the observed is 0.08. The assay had an
order of magnitude more resolving power than the effect required.

## 6. Ceiling on the ML target — how close are the models to the assay limit?

Neither column above is on `mean_log2_ratio_a_over_b`, the quantity `genomic_ml` predicts, so
neither can be set against a cv_strain R² directly. `ml_target_ceiling.py` applies the same
replicate logic to that target, over exactly the 1465 pairs in 20260630's modeling set, in
genomic_ml's convention (R² vs. predicting no winner). Table: `outputs/r04_ml_target_ceiling.csv`.

| | n | R² | ρ |
|---|---|---|---|
| replicate → replicate (1 well predicts 1 well) | 244 | 0.931 | 0.968 |
| ceiling if every label were a mean of 2 wells | 244 | 0.981 | 0.991 |
| **ceiling at the actual replicate mix** | 1465 | **0.966** | **0.983** |
| ceiling from read depth alone (g02 binomial, conservative) | 1465 | 0.897 | 0.947 |
| model: cv_pair, best (xgboost_raw_ko) | 4395 | 0.876 | 0.938 |
| model: cv_strain, best (two_stage_ridge) | 836 | 0.321 | 0.576 |

**cv_pair is essentially at the assay ceiling.** R² 0.876 against a conservative ceiling of 0.897
and an optimistic one of 0.966 — there is almost nothing left to win in that regime, and what
little remains is measurement noise, not biology. This is a second, independent way of saying
what the cv_pair/cv_strain split already says: that regime is a per-strain competitiveness
lookup and it has saturated.

**cv_strain is nowhere near it.** R² 0.321 against 0.897 leaves a gap of ~0.58 that label noise
cannot explain. Whatever is limiting generalization to unseen strains, **it is not the assay**.
That corroborates the replicate learning curve from the other direction: more replicates and more
reads move this number by ~0.01, because they buy down a noise term worth at most 0.10 in total.
More genomes remains the only lever with room in it.

Three numbers, not one, because they answer different questions:

- *replicate → replicate* (0.931) is the literal "what do replicates achieve against each other",
  but it is **not** the ceiling — both sides carry a full dose of noise, while a model is scored
  against the averaged label. It understates.
- *ceiling at the actual mix* (0.966) is the one to quote for this dataset. It matters that only
  244 of 1465 pairs have a second well: 83% of labels are single wells carrying twice the
  variance, so a ceiling read off the replicated pairs alone (0.981) is not this dataset's.
- *binomial* (0.897) comes from read counts rather than replication, covers all 1465 pairs, and
  is not selected for agreement — so where the two disagree, prefer it. The replicate estimate
  is optimistic by construction: `unstable_replicate` pairs are dropped upstream, which removes
  exactly the pairs whose wells disagreed most.

The ρ column for the ceiling rows is the attenuation bound √(reliability), not a measured
correlation.

## Caveats

- Sequencing figures rest on 413 / 453 pairs with ≥2 sequenced wells, versus ~4000 for the
  plate reader — most wells never reach the read threshold. The matched-pair table is the fair
  comparison; the raw per-modality tables are not.
- OD and sequencing measure different things (total biomass vs. composition), so "sequencing is
  more reproducible" is about each as a measurement, not evidence that one is more informative.
- Split-half with 3 replicates compares well 1 against the mean of 2, which is slightly
  favourable versus a strict 1-vs-1; applied identically to both experiments.
