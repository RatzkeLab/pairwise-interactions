# Technical-replicate reproducibility — 20260630 vs 20260721

Baseline for reading every cross-experiment number. Generated by `replicate_report.py`;
tables in `outputs/`, figure in `outputs/figures/t01_replicate_reproducibility.png`.

**These are cross-plate replicates.** 96% of pairs with ≥2 wells have them on *different*
destination plates, so this measures the whole pipeline — pipetting, plate, position, reader
session — not well-to-well noise on one plate. Replicate counts: 20260630 has 2985 pairs at
2× and 1020 at 3×; 20260721 has 2190 at 2× and 1551 at 3×.

20260721's sequencing uses the post-`MIN_RESOLVABLE_BP` re-run (`relative_abundance_refix`).

## 1. Both assays are reproducible — and sequencing beats the plate reader

Split-half agreement (first replicate vs mean of the rest), on the **same set of pairs** for
both modalities:

| | plate reader OD600 | sequencing (rel. abundance) |
|---|---|---|
| **20260630** | ρ = 0.869 | **ρ = 0.928** (median \|Δ\| = 0.040) |
| **20260721** | ρ = 0.686 | **ρ = 0.825** (median \|Δ\| = 0.087) |

Sequencing wins in both experiments despite only ~40 reads per well. A replicate pair of wells
agrees on relative abundance to within 0.04 (20260630) or 0.09 (20260721).

## 2. 20260721 is the noisier experiment, in both modalities independently

OD 0.686 vs 0.869; sequencing 0.825 vs 0.928. Two unrelated measurements agree that 20260721
is the lower-quality run. Any comparison involving it inherits that.

## 3. Wavelength barely matters — OD600 is fine

Across all 61 wavelengths (350–950 nm, 10 nm steps):

| wavelength | 20260630 ρ | 20260721 ρ | median raw OD (20260630) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 350 nm | 0.873 | 0.683 | 1.124 |
| **410 nm (best)** | **0.878** | **0.692** | 0.753 |
| 500 nm | 0.873 | 0.688 | 0.490 |
| **600 nm (convention)** | 0.869 | 0.687 | 0.345 |
| 700 nm | 0.863 | 0.685 | 0.257 |
| 950 nm (worst) | 0.835 | 0.668 | 0.189 |

The curve is nearly flat: 410 nm is best but beats 600 nm by only **+0.009 / +0.005**. Raw
signal falls ~6× from 350 to 900 nm while reproducibility falls ~5%, so signal magnitude is not
the limiting factor — well-to-well biological and handling variation is. **No reason to switch
from OD600**; if you want the marginal gain, 410 nm is the pick, though blue wavelengths pick up
media absorbance as well as scattering.

## 4. Sequencing depth is the lever that does matter

Stratified by each pair's *minimum* well depth (a replicate comparison is only as good as its
shallower member):

| pair min depth | 20260630 ρ | 20260721 ρ | 20260721 median \|Δ\| |
|---|---|---|---|
| <25 reads | 0.920 (n=68) | 0.723 (n=192) | 0.124 |
| 25–45 | 0.916 (n=165) | 0.885 (n=169) | 0.067 |
| ≥45 | 0.969 (n=57) | 0.905 (n=82) | 0.057 |

20260630 is already near ceiling at any depth. **20260721 gains a lot from depth** — ρ 0.72 → 0.91,
and error halves — because 43% of its replicate pairs sit below 25 reads. More reads per well is
the highest-value change for a repeat experiment.

## 5. What this does to the cross-experiment result

The shared-pair cross-experiment correlation was **ρ = 0.084**. Attenuation from measurement
noise alone would cap a perfect match at roughly √(0.869 × 0.686) ≈ **0.77**.

So the cross-experiment comparison is *not* noise-limited. If the two experiments' wells held
the same pairs, the expected correlation is ~0.77 and the observed is 0.08. The assay had an
order of magnitude more resolving power than the effect required.

## Caveats

- Sequencing figures rest on 413 / 453 pairs with ≥2 sequenced wells, versus ~4000 for the
  plate reader — most wells never reach the read threshold. The matched-pair table is the fair
  comparison; the raw per-modality tables are not.
- OD and sequencing measure different things (total biomass vs. composition), so "sequencing is
  more reproducible" is about each as a measurement, not evidence that one is more informative.
- Split-half with 3 replicates compares well 1 against the mean of 2, which is slightly
  favourable versus a strict 1-vs-1; applied identically to both experiments.
